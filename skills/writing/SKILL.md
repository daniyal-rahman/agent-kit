# Writing for a reader
This applies to anything written for someone else, and to long reports to the owner.

A reply, an email, a deck or a doc is an interface, and the reader's experience
of it is yours to design. Work out where the reader starts, what they are
thinking at each point, and what they need next, then arrange the piece so each
part answers the question the last one raised. The reader is busy and will skim.
Whatever you leave them to do (hunt for the point, decode a term, work out what
you want from them) is load you could have removed.

That usually means the point or the ask comes first, each paragraph or slide
carries one thing and opens with it, the name, date or number replaces a
description of it, the reason sits next to its claim, and the piece ends with
what the reader should do next. Cut what this reader doesn't need, even if it
took work to find. Results go in readable figures with a takeaway title, axes
and units.

Before a deck, a doc or an email that matters goes to the owner, a fresh
reviewer reads it as its audience. The reviewer gets the whole piece (rendered,
the way the reader will see it), the task in the owner's words, and who the
reader is. It works out how busy that reader is and what they care about, skims
the piece as that person, and says where it got lost or had to work. Use a
separate reviewer for each distinct kind of reader. Run this review last, on the
version you will send, and again after any fix that changes the content:
correctness fixes add caveats, rows and terms, so a deck can get more correct and
harder to follow in the same round. A fresh review catches a lot, but in one
test it fully caught only 1 of 8 things the owner later corrected; for a deck,
show the owner a one-screen storyboard before building the slides, while the
purpose and order are still cheap to change.

If this audience has seen accepted work before (the last deck, the last report),
open it first and match its style and depth.

When a message goes out under the owner's name, the `voice` skill says how to
write it from their own messages.
