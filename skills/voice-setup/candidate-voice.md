# Writing as the owner

Use this when you draft something the owner will send under their own name. A
description of someone's style doesn't make a model sound like them; their real
messages do. So copy from the samples, not from your sense of a good email.

1. Open `~/.agents/voice/library/INDEX.tsv`. Pick 3 to 5 samples closest to this
   piece: same kind (short reply, scheduling, cold email, thank-you, update,
   explanation) and same relationship (stranger, colleague, boss, family).
   Read them in full, and also `~/.agents/voice/edits.md` if it exists.
2. Take the single closest sample as a template and write the new message as an
   edit of it: same greeting form (often no name), same sign-off, same layout,
   same length, same register. Then swap in the new content.
3. Write only what the reader doesn't already know. The task you were given is
   background for you, not text for them: don't restate what they asked, why
   you're writing, or how long it's been. Keep the owner's shorthand for
   things both sides know ("the 20th", "16-20th", "the deck").
4. Leave the owner's register alone. If their messages to this kind of reader
   run sentences together, use lowercase "i", "u", "--" or no sign-off, do the
   same. Don't tidy their grammar or structure; do fix outright typos.
5. Use only words and moves the samples use. The giveaways of an AI draft: a
   polite opener ("Thanks for getting back to me", "glad you liked"), a
   reassurance ("either way is fine"), a closing offer the samples don't make,
   "First/Second" structure, bullets, em dashes, "not X but Y", words the
   owner never uses ("quite", "apologies", "genuinely", "excited to").
6. Read your draft next to the template. Anything there that the template
   doesn't have a reason for, cut. Hand over only the draft.

When the owner edits a draft before sending, save the sent version as a new
sample in the library (same header format, `ai_drafted: edited`), and add one
line to `~/.agents/voice/edits.md`: what you wrote, what they changed it to.
